Education to Employment: Singapore's Graduate Job Market

How have graduate numbers changed in Singapore, and what does that mean for students choosing what to study? Our group explored graduate supply, labour-market demand and qualification outcomes using public Singapore datasets from 2015 to 2024. My focus was university graduate supply and Power BI visualisation.

PythonPandasGoogle ColabPower BIData VisualisationGroup project · 2026

01 / THE PROJECT

Looking beyond graduate numbers.

Our team explored whether different post-secondary pathways were associated with different job opportunities and outcomes in Singapore. We looked at university and polytechnic graduate supply, vacancies, employment, salaries and qualification outcomes, using a common analysis window of 2015–2024.

The wider project used seven public datasets. Each group member worked on a different area of the analysis. I was responsible for university graduate supply, and also helped with the team slides and data exploration.

03 / OUR METHOD

Making the datasets comparable.

Our group combined seven public datasets from Singapore government sources. We inspected and cleaned them in Google Colab using Python and pandas, then prepared five main analytical datasets for Power BI. To support comparisons, course, degree, industry and occupation labels were mapped into 11 common clusters, with approximately 292 mappings. We checked missing values, corrected types and applied filters to avoid double counting.

07 / GROUP FINDINGS

What our wider analysis found.

My university dashboard was one part of a broader investigation. The other dashboards provided context about diploma pathways, job vacancies, salary and employment outcomes.

Polytechnic graduate supply

The team's polytechnic analysis found that graduate numbers decreased from around 37,000 in 2015 to around 28,000 in 2024. Engineering Sciences and Business & Administration accounted for large shares of the graduate supply.

Dashboard analysis by Ming Kai

Labour-market demand

Job vacancies rose sharply around the post-pandemic recovery before easing by 2024. The team also compared reported vacancies against graduates, while noting that not every vacancy represents a graduate-level position or maps directly to a field of study.

Dashboard analysis by Saniya

University graduate outcomes

Starting salaries generally increased across fields, but employment rates did not always improve alongside them. The analysis showed why salary and employment should be considered together when judging outcomes.

Dashboard analysis by Shin Ren

Diploma versus degree outcomes

Degree holders generally appeared more often in higher income bands and had relatively resilient unemployment outcomes. However, the income comparison includes workers of different ages and experience levels, so it does not isolate the effect of qualification alone.

Dashboard analysis by Kai Kiat

Our conclusion

No single post-secondary pathway is best for everyone. Graduate supply, vacancies, salaries and employment rates each answer different questions. Students should consider the field of study and qualification together, alongside their interests and goals. Our findings are descriptive, not predictions of future job demand.

08 / REFLECTION

What I learned.

This project gave me more experience cleaning real-world data and presenting results in Power BI. I also learned why datasets need consistent labels before they can be compared, and why a rise in graduates does not automatically mean there are more jobs available.

Working as a team helped me see how education data, graduate outcomes and labour demand can contribute different perspectives to the same question.
